# Cybersecurity & Emotional Response Research

## Final Year Project — Cybersecurity and Forensic Computing

My Final Year Project investigated the relationship between **cybersecurity incidents, the personality traits of an individual, and how their emotional response varies following a cyber attack**.

The project originally began following a broader focus on cyber threats, including topics such as the Deep and Dark Web and how the cyber attacks impacted businesses. However, as I went through the research phase, I refined the scope to focus specifically on the emotional factors of cybersecurity, examining how individuals with different personality characteristics respond emotionally to cyber incidents.

The project combined academic research with primary data collection and statistical and qualitative analysis. Allowing me to investigate cybersecurity from a perspective beyond purely technical controls, considering how human behaviour and psychological responses can influence the impact of security incidents.

---

## Project Objectives

The final objectives of the project were to:

* Investigate how individuals with different personality traits respond to cyber incidents.
* Examine the emotional impact experienced following a cyber attack.
* Investigate whether personality characteristics influence perceived control and emotional response.
* Collect primary data through a structured questionnaire.
* Apply established psychological measurement frameworks to cybersecurity research.
* Analyse both quantitative and qualitative responses.
* Compare the findings against existing cybersecurity and psychological research.
* Identify implications for cybersecurity awareness and education.

---

## Research Methodology

The project used a mixed-methods research approach where it combined both quantitative and qualitative data.

A questionnaire was developed to collect responses from participants regarding their experiences and perceptions of cyber incidents. The research incorporated the Impact of Event Scale–Revised (IES-R) alongside the Big Five personality traits to investigate emotional responses to cyber incidents.

Quantitative data was analysed to identify statistical relationships and patterns within participant responses, while qualitative responses were analysed thematically to identify recurring experiences and behaviours.

The project therefore required me to work across several stages:

Research → Questionnaire Design → Ethical Data Collection → Data Processing → Statistical Analysis → Qualitative Analysis → Interpretation → Conclusions

---

## Data Analysis

The quantitative analysis identified two prominent dimensions within participants' responses:

* Emotional Impact
* Perceived Control

The research also identified patterns suggesting that personality characteristics, particularly Conscientiousness and Imaginative tendencies, were associated with variations in emotional impact and perceived control.

Qualitative responses provided further context to the statistical findings. Participants frequently described emotions such as distress or frustration following cyber incidents, where following this many respondents also reported increased cybersecurity awareness and changes in their behaviour afterwards.

---

## Key Findings

The research indicated that cyber incidents should not be viewed purely as technical events.

Participants commonly reported emotional and behavioural consequences following incidents, including:

* Distress and frustration
* Reduced trust in online platforms
* Increased awareness of cybersecurity
* Changes in online behaviour
* Feelings associated with loss of control
* Different emotional responses depending on individual characteristics

The findings suggested that individual personality and interpretation of an incident can influence how a person experiences and responds to a cyber attack.

The study also identified a relationship between cybersecurity incidents and longer-term behavioural adaptation, with many participants reporting increased awareness following an incident.

## Legal, Ethical & Professional Considerations

Because the project involved collecting information about individuals' experiences of cyber incidents, data protection and research ethics were an important part of the project.

The research considered:

* UK GDPR
* Data Protection Act 2018
* Participant consent
* Anonymous data collection
* Confidentiality
* Right to withdraw
* Responsible data handling
* Research integrity
* Avoidance of bias

The project required participants to be informed about how their responses would be used before submitting questionnaire responses.

## Project Management

The project was managed using the **MoSCoW prioritisation framework**, allowing requirements to be separated into Must Have, Should Have, Could Have and Won't Have categories.

The scope of the project changed considerably as the research progressed. Rather than continuing with an overly broad cybersecurity topic, I refined the research question to create a more focused and achievable study.

This required adapting the research methodology and analysis process when challenges arose. For example, the original plan involved using NVivo for qualitative analysis, but software issues resulted in ATLAS.ti being adopted instead.

---

## Limitations

The project had several limitations.

The final questionnaire received **40 responses**, which provided usable data but limited the statistical generalisability of the findings. Additionally, the research relied on self-reported responses, meaning participants' answers could be influenced by individual interpretation or recall bias.

These limitations were considered when interpreting the findings rather than treating the results as universally representative.

## Conclusion

This project allowed me to investigate cybersecurity from a **human-centred perspective**, complementing the technical experience demonstrated by my Wazuh SIEM and Enterprise Ubuntu Home Lab projects.

The research demonstrated that cybersecurity incidents can have consequences extending beyond technical compromise, influencing emotions, perceptions, behaviour and cybersecurity awareness.

The project also developed my ability to design and manage an independent research project, collect and analyse primary data, adapt methodology when challenges occurred, and communicate findings using evidence-based analysis.

Overall, the project strengthened my understanding of the **human factors surrounding cybersecurity** while developing transferable skills in research, statistical analysis, qualitative analysis, project management and security analysis.
